Receipts can already be verified by anyone who holds them. A counterparty in a cross-company action often needs more: the execution's current state, whether its effect is still unresolved, and whether value has settled. Contract 0.2.0 adds **execution shares** for that.

<Note>
  These operations are in customer contract 0.2.0. Confirm they are enabled in your deployment. See [environment and release status](/get-started/environment).
</Note>

## Enroll the counterparty

The counterparty is a principal in your tenant, typically `kind: organization`, signing in through its own enrolled identity provider. Reads stay inside your tenant; there is no cross-tenant query.

## Share one execution

`POST /v1/executions/{execution_id}/shares` names the recipient, a purpose, an expiry, and the views it may see: `state`, `result`, `receipts`, `settlement`. A share is read-only. It gives the recipient no authority over the execution, so it cannot create actions or vote because of it.

## What the counterparty reads

`GET /v1/execution-shares/{share_id}/view` returns only the granted members, as record references computed when it is read. The recipient fetches receipt bodies with `GET /v1/receipts/{receipt_id}?share_id=…`, which succeeds only for records that share covers, and can verify them independently. Expired or revoked shares stop answering.

## Keep the disclosure on the record

Creating a share, the recipient's first read each day, and revoking it are receipts on the execution's stream, so you can prove what the counterparty was shown and when. Revoke with `POST /v1/execution-shares/{share_id}/revocations`.
