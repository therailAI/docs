When an agent acts on behalf of your organization toward a supplier, customer, or auditor, they cannot inspect your internal logs, and they have no reason to trust them. This page shows how the Rail produces evidence designed to leave your organization: records a counterparty can check against a trust anchor they established themselves.

<Note>
  Offline verification uses the Rail CLI supplied for your platform. See [Export and verify evidence](/cli/evidence).
</Note>

## Record every decision as a receipt

Assertions, decisions, and outcomes are written as immutable receipts. Revocation changes future permission but never rewrites a historical receipt. See [Evidence and verification](/concepts/evidence).

## Commit receipts to a checkpointed ledger

Each receipt is signed and included in an append-only ledger with checkpoints and inclusion proofs. Verification keys and checkpoints are available through the evidence API. See [Read the evidence](/guides/verify-evidence#read-the-evidence).

## Hand over a bundle, not access

An evidence bundle packages the connected receipts for a journey. The counterparty runs `rail verify` offline with a trust file obtained through an independent channel; they never need an account in your environment. See [Inspect a recorded journey](/guides/verify-evidence#inspect-a-recorded-journey).

## Scope what a check establishes

Verification dispositions, projections, and effect decisions each answer a narrower question than "is everything fine?". A complete projection does not authorize result release, retries, or settlement on its own. See [Keep verification separate from release](/guides/verify-evidence#keep-verification-separate-from-release).

<Note>
  A valid signature establishes integrity under a trusted key. It does not prove that every external claim is true or that a provider actually moved money.
</Note>
