Money movement is the core function of the Rail platform. Every financial operation — whether a customer is funding their account from a bank, sending crypto to an external wallet, converting USD to ETH, or collecting a payment from a client — is represented by one of five transaction types. Each type has its own API resource, lifecycle states, and settlement mechanics. Understanding when to use each type will help you design the right integration for your use case.

## The Five Transaction Types

```text
External World                Rail Platform
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Bank / Crypto Network ──────► Account          [Deposit]
                    Account ──────────────────► Bank / Wallet   [Withdrawal]
                    Account ──────────────────► Account         [Transfer]
                    Account (Asset A) ─────────► Account (Asset B) [Exchange]
Counterparty ───────────────► Account          [Payment]
```

## Deposits

A **deposit** is money coming in from the outside world into a customer's Rail account. The customer (or a third party) sends funds via a supported payment rail, and Rail credits the account once the funds settle.

**Supported inbound rails:**

| Rail | Asset Type | Typical Settlement |
|---|---|---|
| ACH | Fiat (USD) | 1–3 business days |
| FEDWIRE | Fiat (USD) | Same day |
| SWIFT | Fiat (USD) | 1–2 business days |
| On-chain | Crypto (ETH, BTC, USDC, etc.) | Varies by confirmation requirement |

**When to use deposits:**
- A customer wants to fund their Rail wallet from their personal bank account
- You are building a crypto on-ramp and need to accept ACH payments before converting to crypto
- A business wants to receive SWIFT wires from international clients

See the [Deposits Guide](/guides/deposits) and [Deposits API Reference](/api-reference/deposits).

## Withdrawals

A **withdrawal** moves funds outbound from a customer's Rail account to a verified **counterparty** — a bank account or crypto wallet that has passed compliance verification.

**Supported outbound rails:**

| Rail | Asset Type | Typical Settlement |
|---|---|---|
| ACH | Fiat (USD) | 1–3 business days |
| FEDWIRE | Fiat (USD) | Same day |
| On-chain | Crypto (ETH, BTC, USDC, etc.) | Varies by network congestion |

**When to use withdrawals:**
- A customer wants to cash out their balance to their bank account
- A user wants to withdraw crypto to their self-custody wallet
- You are building a crypto off-ramp and need to pay out fiat after selling digital assets

<Warning>
  Withdrawals can only be sent to counterparties with `ACTIVE` status. Register and verify counterparties before your customers need to withdraw. See [Counterparties](/concepts/counterparties).
</Warning>

See the [Withdrawals Guide](/guides/withdrawals) and [Withdrawals API Reference](/api-reference/withdrawals).

## Transfers

A **transfer** moves funds between two accounts that both exist on the Rail platform. No external payment rail is involved, which makes transfers instant and fee-efficient.

**Common transfer scenarios:**
- Moving USD from one customer's account to another customer's account on your platform
- Rebalancing funds between two of the same customer's accounts (e.g., from a business operating account to a reserve account)
- Internal platform sweep operations

**When to use transfers:**
- Both the source and destination accounts are on Rail
- You need immediate, same-ledger settlement without external rail fees or delays
- You are building a platform wallet or marketplace that moves funds between users internally

<Tip>
  Transfers are the fastest and lowest-cost way to move funds on Rail because settlement is purely ledger-based with no external rails involved.
</Tip>

See the [Transfers & Exchanges Guide](/guides/transfers-exchanges) and [Transfers API Reference](/api-reference/transfers).

## Exchanges

An **exchange** converts funds from one asset type to another within the Rail platform. This covers fiat-to-crypto conversions, crypto-to-fiat conversions, and crypto-to-crypto swaps.

**Common exchange scenarios:**
- A customer deposits USD and wants to buy ETH (fiat → crypto)
- A customer sells USDC and wants to receive USD in their fiat account (crypto → fiat)
- A crypto-to-crypto swap between ETH and USDC

**When to use exchanges:**
- You are building a crypto on-ramp (fiat → crypto) or off-ramp (crypto → fiat)
- Your platform offers multi-asset wallets and customers need to rebalance between assets
- You need to automate currency conversion as part of a payment or payout flow

<Note>
  Exchanges require both a source account and a destination account on Rail. The source account holds the asset being sold, and the destination account receives the purchased asset. Make sure both accounts exist and are `OPEN` before initiating an exchange.
</Note>

See the [Transfers & Exchanges Guide](/guides/transfers-exchanges) and [Exchanges API Reference](/api-reference/exchanges).

## Payments

A **payment** (or collection) is an inbound transaction where Rail pulls funds from a counterparty into a customer's account. Unlike a deposit, where the customer initiates the send from their bank, a payment is Rail-initiated — your platform instructs Rail to collect funds from a verified counterparty.

**Common payment scenarios:**
- A business collects recurring subscription fees from customers via ACH pull
- A marketplace collects a seller's payout source funds on a scheduled basis
- A platform auto-debits a linked bank account when a customer's wallet balance drops below a threshold

**When to use payments:**
- You need to pull funds from a customer's linked bank account (ACH debit)
- You are building a subscription billing or recurring payment product
- You want to automate fund collection rather than waiting for customers to push funds in

See the [Payments & Collections Guide](/guides/payments-collections) and [Payments API Reference](/api-reference/payments).

## Choosing the Right Transaction Type

Use this decision table to quickly identify which transaction type applies to your use case:

| Scenario | Transaction Type |
|---|---|
| Customer funds account from their bank | Deposit |
| Customer withdraws to their bank | Withdrawal |
| Customer withdraws to their crypto wallet | Withdrawal |
| Move funds between two Rail accounts | Transfer |
| Convert USD to ETH within Rail | Exchange |
| Convert ETH to USDC within Rail | Exchange |
| Pull funds from a customer's linked bank | Payment |
| Accept ACH push from an external bank | Deposit |

## Transaction Lifecycle

All five transaction types follow a similar high-level lifecycle, though specific states differ by type:

```text
  CREATED / INITIATED
        │
        ▼
    PENDING  ──► (processing / settlement in progress)
        │
        ▼
   COMPLETED  (funds settled)
        │
   or FAILED  (funds returned / reversed)
```

<Tip>
  Subscribe to [webhook events](/webhooks/events) to receive real-time notifications as transactions move through their lifecycle states. Avoid polling the API repeatedly — webhooks are the most efficient way to track transaction status changes.
</Tip>

## Prerequisites Checklist

Before initiating any money movement, ensure you have completed the following:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Onboard a Customer">
    The customer must have an approved Application and an `ACTIVE` Customer record. See [Customer Onboarding](/guides/customer-onboarding).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open an Account">
    The source (and destination, for transfers and exchanges) account must be in `OPEN` status with the correct `asset_type_id`. See [Account Management](/guides/account-management).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register a Counterparty (withdrawals and payments only)">
    For withdrawals and payments, the external bank account or wallet must be registered as an `ACTIVE` counterparty. See [Counterparties](/concepts/counterparties).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Initiate the Transaction">
    Call the appropriate API endpoint with the account ID, amount, and any required counterparty or destination account references.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Listen for Webhooks">
    Subscribe to transaction events to track status changes in real time. See [Webhook Events](/webhooks/events).
  </Step>
</Steps>
