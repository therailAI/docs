---
title: "Authority and delegation"
description: "Bound permission to actor, purpose, scope, time, and governing conditions."
---

A grant identifies the principal and the conditions under which it may act. Scope can constrain action classes, resources, purpose, audience, data classes, regions, validity windows, delegation depth, and permitted use.

A child grant or delegation can only narrow existing authority. Delegation does not erase the parent chain or turn a technically reachable resource into an authorized one.

## Approval is not authority expansion

Human approval applies to the exact action and policy context presented for review. Reaching quorum triggers fresh evaluation. It does not override policy or revive revoked authority.

## Revocation is prospective

After revocation, later protected effects must re-evaluate the relevant authority. Earlier evidence remains unchanged. Stopping an agent process and ending its authority are different operations.

## Exclusive use and reservations

A reservation binds a permitted use to a holder and scope. Expiry or a crashed process does not automatically make that authority available again when an external effect may have occurred. Reconciliation must establish whether release or consumption is justified.

At-most-one effective reservation does not guarantee universal exactly-once behavior in an external system. Your adapter and provider must supply their own effect-idempotency and reconciliation behavior.
