title: "Verify evidence independently"
description: "Inspect records without confusing integrity with authority or external truth."
---

## Read the evidence

An execution exposes receipt IDs. Use [`getReceipt`](/api-reference/endpoints/get-receipt), its inclusion proof, verification keys, and checkpoints as applicable to the verification profile. Sensitive object access remains subject to current authorization.

The customer API also exposes receipt-verification requests, verification dispositions, projections, and effect decisions. Each has a different role; see [the evidence model](/concepts/evidence).

## Inspect a recorded journey

Open one of the [six recorded workflows](/examples/recorded-workflows). The workbench displays a bundle and a **supplied verifier report**. Viewing that report is not running a verifier in your browser.

Download the evidence bundle and inspect it with the Rail CLI supplied for your platform:

```bash
rail bundle inspect journey.bundle.json
rail verify journey.bundle.json --trust-file trust.json --json
```

Obtain and confirm the trust anchor through an independent trusted channel before relying on the result. An accompanying `trust.json` is useful input, but its location alongside the bundle does not itself establish trust.

## Interpret the result

Check signature and commitment validation, causal linkage, and the applicable verification profile. Current-time authority can be stale even when the historical signed record remains intact. Do not reinterpret historical validity as permission for new execution.

A cryptographic check cannot establish that every external claim is true, that a provider actually moved live money, or that the deployment is production-ready.

## Keep verification separate from release

A complete scoped projection does not authorize every effect. Result release, registry promotion, retry, and settlement each require their applicable authority and current decision.

See [CLI usage](/sdks/cli) for obtaining and using a matching verifier.
